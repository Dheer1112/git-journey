What is a TPU?

Tensor Processing Units are application-specific integrated circuits (ASICs) designed by Google specifically for neural network workloads.

⚡

Systolic Arrays

Unlike general-purpose GPUs, TPUs use massive systolic arrays optimized for matrix multiplications — the core of deep learning.

🔗

Inter-Chip Interconnect

Custom high-bandwidth, low-latency networking (ICI) lets thousands of chips act as a single coherent supercomputer.

🌱

Power Efficiency

Purpose-built silicon delivers dramatically better performance-per-watt than traditional GPUs for tensor operations.

A Decade of TPU Evolution

From a research prototype powering AlphaGo to multi-exaflop superpods.

2015 – 2016

TPU v1

Inference-only. 28 nm process. Powered AlphaGo’s historic victory. Up to 92 TOPS of 8-bit operations.

2017 – 2018

TPU v2 & v3

First training-capable generations. Introduction of High Bandwidth Memory and 2D torus interconnect.

2021 – 2023

TPU v4 & v5

Optical circuit switches, larger pods (up to 8,960 chips), SparseCores for embedding-heavy workloads.

2024

TPU v6e — Trillium

Strong focus on cost and energy efficiency. 918 BF16 TFLOPs per chip, 256-chip pods.

2025 – 2026

TPU v7 — Ironwood

First TPU designed for the age of inference. 4,614 FP8 TFLOPs, 192 GB HBM, 9,216-chip superpods.

2026 (Announced)

TPU 8t & 8i

First split architecture: dedicated training (8t) and inference/reasoning (8i) chips for the agentic era.

Ironwood — 7th Generation

Generally available. Google’s most powerful and energy-efficient TPU to date.

Generally Available

Built for the Inference Era

Ironwood delivers more than 4× better performance per chip for both training and inference compared to Trillium, while offering 2× better performance-per-watt. A single superpod scales to 9,216 chips.

8th Generation — Two Chips for the Agentic Era

Announced April 2026. Purpose-built architectures for training and inference.

🏋️
Training

TPU 8t (Sunfish)

Designed with Broadcom. Optimized for large-scale pre-training and embedding-heavy workloads. Reduces frontier model development from months to weeks.

  • Up to 9,600 chips per superpod
  • ~2 PB shared high-bandwidth memory
  • 121 ExaFlops peak compute
  • Double inter-chip bandwidth vs Ironwood
  • Up to 2.7× better performance-per-dollar for training
  • Native FP4 / block-scale multiplication
🚀
Inference & Reasoning

TPU 8i (Zebrafish)

Designed with MediaTek. Built for low-latency serving, agentic workflows, and Mixture-of-Experts models. Breaks the memory wall.

  • 288 GB HBM + 384 MB on-chip SRAM
  • Collectives Acceleration Engine (CAE)
  • Up to 80% better performance-per-dollar
  • 2× better performance-per-watt vs Ironwood
  • Reduced network diameter for MoE all-to-all
  • Optimized for sampling, RL & high-concurrency serving

Why This Matters for AI / ML

Hardware specialization is accelerating the entire AI stack.

🧠

Faster Frontier Models

Training timelines compressed from months to weeks enable more experimentation and rapid iteration on next-generation models.

🤖

Agentic Workflows

Low-latency, high-concurrency inference is essential for multi-step agent systems that reason, plan, and act in real time.

💰

Better Economics

Dramatic gains in performance-per-dollar and performance-per-watt make large-scale AI more accessible and sustainable.

⚖️

Competition with GPUs

TPUs close the gap with NVIDIA flagships while offering unique scale advantages through custom interconnects and pods.

🧩

Software Co-design

JAX, Pathways, and the AI Hypercomputer stack are tightly integrated with TPU hardware for maximum utilization.

🌍

Energy & Scale

With power becoming a primary constraint, efficiency gains of 2× per generation compound into massive real-world impact.

The Road Ahead

The split between training and inference silicon marks a new chapter. As models grow denser, more multimodal, and more agentic, specialized accelerators like the TPU family will continue to define what is possible.
