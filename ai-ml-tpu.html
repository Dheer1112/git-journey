Foundations

AI/ML, in the terms that matter for hardware

Before talking chips, it helps to be precise about what's actually running on them.

Machine Learning

Learning weights from data

Instead of hand-coding rules, a model adjusts millions to trillions of numeric parameters until its outputs match examples it's shown. Training is search; inference is applying what was found.

Deep Learning

Layers of linear algebra

A neural network is a chain of matrix multiplications separated by simple nonlinear functions. Depth lets it represent increasingly abstract patterns — edges, then shapes, then concepts.

Training vs Inference

Two very different jobs

Training multiplies enormous matrices over and over while tracking gradients to update weights. Inference just runs the forward pass, once, as fast and cheaply as possible.

The bottleneck

It's arithmetic, at scale

A modern transformer performs trillions of multiply-accumulate operations per forward pass. General-purpose CPUs handle that arithmetic one instruction at a time — far too slowly.

Why Specialized Silicon

CPU, GPU, TPU — three answers to the same problem

Each generation of hardware traded away generality for throughput on the operations AI actually needs.

CPU

General-purpose sequencing
  • Optimized for branching, varied instructions
  • Few cores, high per-core flexibility
  • Wastes silicon on control logic matrix math doesn't need

GPU

Massively parallel, general accelerator
  • Thousands of cores built for graphics-style parallelism
  • Flexible: graphics, simulation, ML, anything parallel
  • Tensor cores added later to chase matmul throughput

TPU

One operation, done relentlessly
  • Built around a systolic array — nothing else
  • No graphics pipeline, no general instruction set to carry
  • Higher throughput per watt on matmul-heavy workloads
Architecture

Inside the systolic array

A TPU's core is a Matrix Multiply Unit (MXU) — a grid of multiply-accumulate cells wired directly to their neighbors. Weights are loaded into the grid once and held stationary.

Activations then flow into the array edge, cell by cell, in rhythm — like a heartbeat, which is where the name "systolic" comes from. Each cell multiplies its input by the stationary weight, adds the running sum from its neighbor, and passes the result along.

Because no intermediate result ever leaves the grid to touch memory, the MXU avoids the data-movement cost that limits general-purpose chips. Early TPUs used 128×128 arrays; Trillium and Ironwood doubled that to 256×256, quadrupling multiply-accumulate throughput per cycle.

A Decade Of Iteration

The TPU generations

Each generation answered a limitation of the last — inference-only, then training-capable, then split by workload entirely.

01

TPU v1

2015

A deterministic inference ASIC built to keep tail latency predictable for search ranking and speech recognition — Google's first proof that custom silicon beat general CPUs for this arithmetic.

02—03

TPU v2 & v3

2017–2018

Added bfloat16 precision, on-chip high-bandwidth memory, and pod-scale interconnects — turning an inference chip into a genuine training platform for the first time.

04

TPU v4

2021

Scaled to 4,096-chip pods and introduced optical circuit switches, letting the compiler treat the interconnect topology itself as a configurable resource.

05

TPU v5e & v5p

2023

Google split the line in two: v5e for cost-efficient, high-volume inference and mid-size training; v5p for frontier-scale training, delivering roughly 2.8× the training speed of v4 across 4,096-chip pods.

06

TPU v6e "Trillium"

2024

Doubled the MXU to 256×256, quadrupling per-cycle throughput, while cutting power draw enough to deliver roughly 67% better energy efficiency than v5e at similar silicon area.

07

TPU v7 "Ironwood"

2025Current flagship

The first TPU with native FP8 support, 192 GB of HBM3e per chip, and a 9,216-chip superpod architecture — closing most of the raw-performance gap with Nvidia's flagship GPUs for the first time.

08

TPU 8t & 8i

2026, expected

Google's next step splits the chip itself by job: 8t tuned for frontier pre-training, 8i for agentic and mixture-of-experts inference at higher memory capacity — the first generation where training and inference get dedicated hardware rather than one chip doing both.

In Production

Where TPUs actually run

Available exclusively through Google Cloud — there's no on-prem version, no separate marketplace.

Frontier model training

Large language model pre-training runs that need thousands of chips acting as one machine, with pod-scale interconnect keeping communication overhead low.

Production inference

Serving models like Claude at scale, where perf-per-watt directly sets the cost of every request.

Search & recommendation

The original TPU workload — ranking and retrieval systems that need predictable low latency across billions of queries.

Generative media

Image and video generation pipelines, where compute-bound diffusion steps benefit from raw matmul throughput.
